Automatic text summarization is the process of reducing a text document with a computer program in order to create a summary that retains the most important points of the original document.
Technologies that can make a coherent summary take into account variables such as length, writing style and syntax.
An example of the use of summarization technology is search engines such as Google.
Document summarization is another.

There are broadly two types of summarization: extractive and abstractive summarization.
Extractive summarization methods work by selecting a subset of existing words, phrases or sentences from the original text to form the summary.
Abstractive summarization methods generate new sentences to describe the main points of the input document.
Abstractive methods build an internal semantic representation and then use natural language generation techniques to create a summary that is closer to what a human might express.

The Luhn heuristic method was one of the earliest approaches to automatic summarization, proposed by Hans Peter Luhn in 1958.
Luhn used the frequency of important words to identify key sentences in a document.
The method assigns a score to each sentence based on the frequency of significant words within it.
Sentences with higher scores are more likely to be included in the final summary.

Latent Semantic Analysis, or LSA, is a mathematical method for extracting and representing the contextual-usage meaning of words by statistical computations applied to a large corpus of text.
LSA captures higher-order relationships between terms and documents using singular value decomposition.
This approach allows LSA-based summarizers to identify important sentences that capture the main topics of a document.

TextRank is a graph-based ranking algorithm for NLP tasks inspired by PageRank.
TextRank builds a graph where sentences are vertices and edges represent similarity between sentences.
The algorithm iterates until convergence to assign importance scores to each sentence.
Sentences with higher scores are selected for the final summary.

LexRank is another graph-based approach that uses the concept of centrality in a graph to determine sentence importance.
It computes sentence similarity using cosine similarity with TF-IDF term vectors.
LexRank selects the most central sentences as the summary.

The KL-Sum method selects sentences that minimize the KL-divergence between the word distribution of the summary and the word distribution of the source document.
It greedily adds sentences to the summary that reduce the divergence at each step.
This statistical approach ensures the summary preserves the overall word distribution of the original text.

The SumBasic algorithm is a simple word-frequency approach to summarization.
SumBasic assigns probabilities to words based on their frequency in the document.
It then selects sentences with words that have the highest average probability.
After each selection, the probabilities of the selected words are updated to penalize repetition.

Evaluation of automatic summarization systems is typically done using the ROUGE metric.
ROUGE stands for Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation.
It measures the overlap between the automatically generated summary and one or more reference summaries.
ROUGE-1 counts unigram overlaps, ROUGE-2 counts bigram overlaps, and ROUGE-L uses the longest common subsequence.
